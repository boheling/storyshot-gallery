# Planting a Flag — sources

> These are the sources for the whole subject, checked for the twelve-scene adult cut of this film.
> The 5th-grade cut in this folder uses a subset: it drops the Requerimiento, *Inter caetera* and
> *Johnson v. M'Intosh* entirely (see `notes.md`), and keeps every hedge recorded below for the
> parts it does show.

Every date, object and number spoken or shown in the film, with where it comes
from and where the sources disagree. Checked November 2026.

---

## The central correction

The crowned letters on the banner Columbus carried are **F** and **Y**, not
Y and K:

- **F** = *Fernando* — Ferdinand II of Aragon
- **Y** = *Ysabel* — the fifteenth-century Spanish spelling of Isabel
  (Isabella I of Castile)

Two letters because Spain in 1492 was two crowns joined by a marriage, not one
kingdom. No source gives a **K**; the likeliest explanation is an ornate drawn
**F** read as a K in a classroom handout.

These were **Spanish**, in 1492. England's claims come a century later and used
different objects entirely (see *Cape Henry* below).

- Flags of the World, Columbus's flags —
  <https://crwflags.com/FoTW/flags/es_colum.html>

*Caveat shown on screen:* the banner's exact design survives only through later
depictions, so the drawing is marked **reconstruction**. FOTW notes that
representations of the 1492 landing are later and probably inaccurate.

## The ceremony

A claim was a set procedure: plant a banner, read a proclamation aloud, perform
a physical act on the land (cut a branch, lift earth), rename the place, and
have a notary record it. The record was the operative part — it was what could
be produced later in Europe. The film marks its composite drawing as
illustrative.

## Dividing the Atlantic

- **Inter caetera**, Pope Alexander VI, May 1493: a north–south line 100 leagues
  west of the Cape Verde islands.
- **Treaty of Tordesillas**, signed 7 June 1494: Portugal objected that 100 was
  too close, and the line moved to **370 leagues** west. Ratified by Spain
  2 July 1494 and by Portugal 5 September 1494.
- England and France were not parties and never accepted it.

- Treaty text — <https://www.let.rug.nl/usa/documents/before-1600/the-treaty-of-tordesillas-june-7-1494.php>
- Overview — <https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/treaty-tordesillas>
- Inter caetera — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_caetera>

*Disagreement:* sources convert 370 leagues to anywhere from ~1,100 to ~1,277
statute miles, because the league was not standardised. **The film says "370
leagues" and gives no mileage.** Sources also differ on whether the papal
sanction of the treaty came in 1505 or 1506.

## Portugal's stone pillars

Portuguese captains carried pre-carved limestone pillars — *padrões* — topped
with the cross of the Order of Christ and cut with the royal arms, the king's
name and a date. Diogo Cão placed several down the west African coast on two
voyages between 1482 and 1486, including at the mouth of the Congo and at
**Cape Cross** in present-day Namibia.

- Diogo Cão — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_C%C3%A3o>
- What a padrão was — <https://worldhistorycommons.org/padrao>

*Disagreement:* the fate of the Congo pillar (destroyed by the Dutch in 1642, or
still standing in ruins) and the date of the Cabo Negro pillar (1483 or 1484).

## The Requerimiento

Drafted by the jurist **Juan López de Palacios Rubios**, **1513**. It was read
aloud before an attack and demanded submission to the Crown and the Church; if
the listeners "refused", the resulting war was deemed lawful and the
conquistadors shielded from liability.

In practice it was read **in Spanish to people who spoke no Spanish**, shouted
across a battlefield, read from ships, read outside villages with nobody in
sight, and read to the backs of people walking away. Historians generally
conclude the real audience was the Spanish themselves.

- Overview — <https://www.worldhistory.org/Spanish_Requirement/>
- Primary document — <https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/el-requerimiento-by-juan-lopez-de-palacios-rubios-1513/>
- Text and translation — <https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/Requerimiento.pdf>

*Disagreement:* a minority of sources date the drafting to 1510. The film uses
1513, the usual attribution.

## France: Cartier at Gaspé

**24 July 1534.** Jacques Cartier raised a cross **thirty feet high** at Gaspé,
with a shield bearing **three fleurs-de-lis** and a board reading **"Vive le Roy
de France"**.

The Iroquoian chief **Donnacona objected**. Cartier told him the cross was only
a landmark. It was not, and Cartier later took Donnacona's two sons to France.

- Fleur-de-lys — <https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/fleur-de-lys>
- Cartier's first voyage — <https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/qc/cartierbrebeuf/culture/histoire-history/evenement-event/cartier/a>
- Biography — <https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/107>

*Disagreement:* one source gives the height as ten metres (~33 ft), against
Cartier's own "thirty feet". The film uses thirty feet.

## England: Cape Henry and Jamestown

- **26 April 1607** — the colonists land at Cape Henry.
- **29 April 1607** — they set up a **cross**, signifying English possession.
- **24 May 1607** — a second cross, inscribed **"Jacobus Rex"** (James, King).
- The Virginia Company was chartered 10 April 1606.

- Jamestown timeline — <https://historicjamestowne.org/history/jamestown-timeline/>
- Cape Henry landing — <https://preservationvirginia.org/cape-henry-lighthouse/moored-in-history-the-english-colonists-first-landing-at-cape-henry-april-26-1607/>

**A claim the film deliberately does not make:** it is often said that a Union
flag ("the King's Colours") was raised at Jamestown in 1607. The only source
found for this was a flag retailer's blog with no primary citation, and the
"King's Colours" name belongs to a later period. The film shows the **crosses**,
which the records do support, and says so on screen.

## Tobacco

**John Rolfe** obtained *Nicotiana tabacum* seed from the Caribbean and was
growing it at Jamestown by **1612**. He called his strain "Orinoco".

Export figures, as reported: **20,000 lb in 1617**, and **40,000 lb in 1618**.

- John Rolfe — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rolfe>
- Tobacco in Colonial Virginia — <https://gutenberg.org/files/27117/27117-h/27117-h.htm>

*Disagreement — significant.* The date of the first shipment is given as 1612,
1613 or 1614 depending on the source, and the early export series do not
reconcile at all (one gives 2,000 lb in 1615 rising to 1.5 million by 1629;
another 2,500 lb in 1616 to 50,000 lb in 1628). **The film uses the
1617/1618 pair and marks the figures as estimates that vary between sources.**

## The closing claims

- **Johnson v. M'Intosh (1823)** brought the "doctrine of discovery" into US
  law: Chief Justice Marshall held that discovery gave title to the European
  government whose subjects made it, leaving Native nations a "right of
  occupancy". It has not been overturned, and the Supreme Court referred to it
  as recently as *City of Sherrill v. Oneida Indian Nation* (2005).
- **30 March 2023** — the Vatican issued a joint statement repudiating the
  doctrine of discovery and *terra nullius*.

- Vatican statement — <https://www.jurist.org/news/2023/03/vatican-rejects-doctrine-of-discovery-following-indigenous-demands/>
- Legal background — <https://blogs.loc.gov/law/2023/07/doctrine-of-discovery-until-otherwise/>

*Disagreement:* sources differ on whether the 2023 statement formally rescinded
the fifteenth-century bulls themselves, or only disavowed the doctrine built on
them. The film says the Vatican "repudiated the doctrine", which is the claim
all the sources support.

## Names

The island Columbus landed on was called **Guanahaní** by the Lucayan people
(a Taíno group) who lived there. He renamed it San Salvador.
